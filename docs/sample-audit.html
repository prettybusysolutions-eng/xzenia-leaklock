Input and scope

Example business: Northstar Software, a fictional subscription company.

Authorized input: A synthetic CSV containing invoice ID, customer ID, due amount, invoice status, retry count, credit note amount, and timestamps.

Question: Which billing events deserve human review before any billing-system change?

Prioritized findings

Finding Observed evidence Interpretation Confidence
Failed invoices with no recorded retry 18 synthetic rows remain failed more than 14 days after due date and show retry_count = 0. These rows are candidates for retry-policy review. The export alone does not prove they are collectible. High signal confidence
Credits exceed the related invoice balance 4 synthetic rows show credit_note_amount greater than due_amount. These rows merit reconciliation against the source ledger. They may reflect valid adjustments or data-quality defects. High signal confidence
Paid status lacks a payment timestamp 7 synthetic rows are marked paid while paid_at is empty. This is an evidence-integrity issue. It is not a revenue-loss claim. Medium signal confidence

Observed facts

  • The listed row conditions exist in the synthetic input.
  • Each finding can be traced to specific synthetic row IDs.
  • No system action was taken.

Not yet proven

  • Whether any invoice is collectible
  • Whether any credit is incorrect
  • Whether any amount is recoverable revenue

Recommended action order

  1. Reconcile the 4 excess-credit candidates against the source ledger.
  2. Confirm the intended retry policy, then review the 18 no-retry candidates.
  3. Repair or explain the 7 missing payment timestamps before using that field for reporting.
  4. Measure outcomes only after authorized remediation and verified settlement.
The real audit uses authorized customer data and delivers an evidence register with traceable findings. It does not promise recovery, make autonomous billing changes, or count an estimate as revenue.
